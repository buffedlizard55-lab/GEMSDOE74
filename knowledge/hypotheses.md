## Why new hypotheses are needed

The incumbent best in the family is **H33-2-B2** (37,654 dots, 0 within 200 m of catalogue) at **0.2778** on the live board **[OWNER-REPORT, GEMSDOE32 docs/index.html]**. Its mechanism is **DTI budget thinning**: DTI = T / (0.2·(T+F) + 0.8·|G|); adding one dot costs 0.2 regardless of k, so break-even is k > 0.2·DTI (≈0.055 at 0.27). A thick surface contains many dots with k < 0.055 (already covered); deleting them raises mean credit per dot. H33-2-B2 does this by deleting every dot within 2 px of the catalogue (the catalogue-masked FP penalty is free in evaluation but expensive live). This is a **packing** improvement, not a **discovery** improvement.

The live leaderboard high is **0.4413** **[OFFICIAL, https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/leaderboard/]** (nchuzhoy, 3 subs), **+58%** over 0.2778. Through the metric's arithmetic, at fixed emitted mass this requires **+58% mean credit per dot**. No amount of re-packing the same field can supply that — the field's spatial ranking must change. The catalogue-difference audit shows GDR QFaults v2 has **only 1 px beyond 300 m of the given catalogue** **[MEASURED, GEMSDOE32 docs/research/h33-hypotheses.md]**; SGMC has **61,664 px beyond 300 m** but the best field enriches SGMC only **1.4× over background** — so a detector that finds the SGMC population is genuinely novel.

Hence the five candidates below all target **faults the catalogue lacks by construction**: those under basin fill, with no preserved scarp, or expressed only as permeability.
