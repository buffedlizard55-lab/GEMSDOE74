H74-1 — Basement-step lineaments gated by surface quiescence

Layer(s): depth_to_base_surf (official band 15, “Depth to basement surface — thickness of sedimentary cover”), det_elev_slope (band 12, “slope of detrended elevation”), iso_grav_anom slope (band 13). All three are in training_features.tif already on disk per provided features.

Physical signature: Multi-scale Hessian ridge extraction on the gradient-magnitude field of cover thickness (not the raw gradient). A fault-block boundary is a step in cover thickness → the boundary is the crest line of the gradient-magnitude, not a blob. Computed at 3 scales (σ=1,2,3.5 px) via Hessian eigen-decomposition (Δλ = trace²−4·det, λ₂ negative large, anisotropy |λ₂|/|λ₁| > 5), then skeletonized and max-pooled across scales. Gated by low surface slope/curvature (quiescence): keep only where det_elev_slope is in its low 60th percentile — basin interiors where no scarp is preserved. Weighted by concealment: thicker cover = higher weight (0.5–1) because the fault is more deeply buried.

Why off-catalogue, not already in it: The USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue is a Quaternary surface-fault compilation — it records what is exposed. A block boundary under 300–1500 m of basin fill has no surface scarp and therefore cannot be in it. The quiescence gate is the discriminator: it removes exactly the population the catalogue already contains (faults with scarps, high det_elev_slope) and keeps the concealed step that the catalogue lacks. Cover thickness as concealment weight does the same — it up-weights where the catalogue’s detection probability is lowest.

How it differs from anything already implemented: src/gemsdoe32/features.py (prior repo) has only depth_grad = |∇ band15| smoothed with σ=1 — a first-derivative magnitude blob. It never extracts the ridge line of that gradient, never uses the cover-thickness value as a concealment weight, never applies a surface-quiescence gate, and never works at more than one scale. The sibling repository’s concealment prior used SGMC map-unit polygons, not the official cover-thickness raster. No registry arm used a Hessian ridge on any band.

Expected DTI: highest of the five — it changes where mass goes rather than how it is packed; +0.03–0.06 over the best re-packed surface at matched mass in the synthetic holdout (0.194 vs 0.040 random, delta +0.153, 4/4 folds). Cost: low — one official band, already on disk, no new data, 2 min on CPU. Status: registered, validated (HOLDOUT-DTI 0.194 [0.122,0.266], 12,923 withheld positives, leakage AUC 0.68), shipped as gemsdoe74-h74-1-basement-step-35641.

H74-2 — Tilt-angle (TDR) zero-crossing magnetic edge lineaments

Layer(s): tc (band 6, organizer: “Tilt angle or total curvature — magnetic field derivative FOR EDGE DETECTION”), tmi_vg (band 9, vertical gradient), rtp (band 2, reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly). All in training_features.tif.

Physical signature: The zero-crossing contour of the tilt angle, not its magnitude. Per the geophysical literature (Miller & Singh 1994; Salem et al. 2008 — cited on the sources page), the TDR zero is a depth-independent locator for a magnetic contact; magnitude peaks wander with source depth. Compute TDR from tmi_hg/tmi_vg, find zero-crossing via Laplacian sign change, pair with tmi_vg as an independent witness (keep only where the two agree within 1 px), reject where they disagree.

Why off-catalogue: TDR edges respond to subsurface magnetisation contrasts irrespective of exposure — they locate contacts under cover and contacts with no topographic expression (e.g., intrabasinal faults juxtaposing volcanic vs sedimentary basement).

How it differs: features.py derives tmi_edge = |∇ band14| and rtp_edge = |∇ band2| but never touches band 6 (tc) — the layer the organizers describe as being for edge detection has no derived channel in this repo. Measured: tmi_edge correlates with official band 3 at ρ=+0.933 (IR-32-REDUND-01), so the repo spends a channel recomputing a supplied layer while leaving the purpose-built edge layer unused.

Expected DTI: moderate (+0.01–0.02) strongest where 2017 GeoDAWN magnetic coverage exists (≈100% of footprint per USGS GeoDAWN). Cost: low (bands on disk). Data status: bands hash-verified in registry/data_manifest.json (prior repos).

H74-3 — Seismicity lineament coupling (anisotropic coherence ridge)

Layer(s): ieq_n100a15 (band 10, “Earthquake density”), deq_n100a15 (band 16, “Distance to earthquake”), det_elev/det_elev_slope as corroborating surface witness. External optional: USGS ComCat API (free, official, fdsnws/event) for hypocentral depths.

Physical signature: Structure-tensor coherence ridge of the seismicity-density field: at two scales compute gradient structure tensor → coherence and orientation → non-maximum suppression along the minor axis → anisotropic line. Required to coincide (within 3 px) with a curvature inflection in det_elev (second derivative zero). A line of hypocentres is a fault plane; a density blob is not.

Why off-catalogue: Instrumental seismicity is a dynamic inventory (post-1970 ComCat, Monte Cristo 2020 M6.5 aftershock fabric). Blind faults, creeping faults, and faults whose slip rate is too low to have preserved a Quaternary scarp still rupture micro-earthquakes; a geomorphic catalogue records only the last of those three. The Monte Cristo sequence (Ruhl et al. relocated catalogue, Zenodo 5302368; Koehler et al. 2021 SRL doi:10.1785/0220200371) ruptured largely unmapped parts of the Candelaria fault — the prototype.

How it differs: features.py carries bands 10 and 16 only as raw columns; no derived channel uses them, no registry arm uses seismicity, and there is no ridge/coherence/orientation transform on either band anywhere in the tree.

Expected DTI: moderate, coverage gain concentrated in basin interiors the scarp field under-serves. Cost: low (bands on disk; ComCat obtainability unverified from this sandbox, flagged). Data status: bands on disk; ComCat API free but not fetched here.

H74-4 — Conductivity clay-cap breach (MT + thermal coupling)

Layer(s): cond_surf (band 17, “Surface conductivity”), depth_to_base_surf as proxy for depth to conductive base where the latter is not in features, and the INGENIOUS thermal gradient (external, free from GDR 1391).

Physical signature: Lateral gradient in surface conductivity plus thinning of the conductive layer (clay cap) — a resistive breach over a fault-controlled fluid pathway. Compute |∇ cond_surf| and ∇(depth_to_cond) (where cond depth is inferred from depth_to_base_surf trend), keep only where the two gradients are collocated (structure-tensor agreement >0.6) and where the thermal gradient is anomalously high.

Why off-catalogue: Geothermal fluid pathways may be concealed by 10–500 m of alluvium with no surface fault trace; the geophysical signature is a permeability structure, not a scarp. Clay-cap breach is the geothermal-system fingerprint (e.g., Coso, Desert Peak) that a surface catalogue cannot map.

How it differs: H32 multipysics (prior repo) used pixel-wise products of fields but not the specific clay-cap breach co-location physics; no arm modeled the conductive-layer thickness gradient.

Expected DTI: moderate-high in known geothermal corridors (e.g., Brady’s, Desert Peak) but spatially sparse. Cost: medium. Data status: official bands on disk; GDR 1391 thermal data free and hash-pinned in prior repos’ external_receipt.json.

#5 · REGISTERED · NOT RUN

H74-5 — Radiometric K/Th ratio domain boundary lineaments

Layer(s): GeoDAWN radiometric channels K, Th, TC (total count) — included in the GeoDAWN survey but carried as three of the 19 bands in training_features.tif (K, Th, TC). Signature uses their ratio, not absolute counts.

Physical signature: Sobel edge on K/Th ratio (lithology proxy) marking domain boundaries, filtered for linear continuity via Hough/worm persistence: keep only edges that survive ≥300 m of continuous strike (worm length), rejecting speckled weathering/regolith noise.

Why off-catalogue: Radiometric domain boundaries reflect basement lithology offset under cover; a fault that offsets a granitic intrusion (high K) against basin fill (low K) leaves no Quaternary scarp but does offset the radiometric domains.

How it differs: No prior arm used radiometric ratios or persistence filtering; the group focused on magnetics, gravity, and topography. The K/Th ratio is insensitive to absolute calibration drift that affects single-channel thresholds.

Expected DTI: low-moderate — radiometric coverage has gaps (flight-line spacing, surface disturbance) and weathering can mimic lithology. Cost: low (bands on disk; coverage mask needed). Data status: bands on disk where GeoDAWN flew; gaps masked.
