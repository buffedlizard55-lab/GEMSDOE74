These are testable ideas, not results or score projections. Official feature rasters and labels are absent from this checkout, so no candidate can presently be evaluated or released. The cited feature field names come from historical project documentation and must be checked against the official data manifest before implementation.

Ranking below combines anticipated orthogonality to the existing synthetic experiments with implementation effort. It is a qualitative prioritization only—not a measured DTI estimate. Off-catalogue rationale is a scientific mechanism, not proof that a particular signal corresponds to a fault.

1 — Magnetic tilt-angle contact continuity

Candidate layers: `tc` (tilt/curvature edge channel), `tmi_vg`, and `rtp`, if present in the official feature stack (historical docs identify these fields; confirm via official manifest).

Signature: zero-crossing contour in tilt-angle response, supported by vertical-gradient sign/edge persistence across scales and agreement in reduced-to-pole magnetic boundaries. Filter components by coherent strike, not just high-amplitude pixels.

Why it could add off-catalogue faults: magnetic susceptibility contrasts can persist beneath basin fill where a geomorphic scarp inventory has no surface expression. Magnetic contacts are non-unique, so only a coherent boundary independently supported by other data is a useful fault candidate.

Difference from repo: current code's H74-1 generator synthesizes a basement-depth field and extracts Hessian ridges; it does not process real magnetic bands. The earlier H74-A path is spring/radiometric association, not tilt-angle zero crossings. Cost: low-to-medium after authorized rasters are available.

Validation state: not run; expected DTI direction unknown. Main confounder: lithologic contacts and residual survey artifacts.

2 — Gravity-gradient curvature / edge persistence

Candidate layers: `iso_grav_anom` and any official gravity derivatives or terrain-corrected products present in the official stack; exact fields are unconfirmed in this checkout.

Signature: multi-scale Laplacian/curvature zero-crossings along persistent horizontal-gradient ridges, with orientation continuity; suppress broad regional fields before edge extraction.

Why it could add off-catalogue faults: density contrasts and basin-bounding structures may reveal buried offsets even where the surface trace is obscured. A gravity edge alone can instead be a basin margin or lithologic boundary.

Difference from repo: repository experiments synthesize depth/topography or use named magnetic/radiometric associations; no real gravity curvature-persistence pipeline is implemented. Cost: medium (feature provenance, scale selection, and mask handling required).

Validation state: not run; expected DTI direction unknown. Main confounder: non-fault density boundaries, including basin fill and intrusive bodies.

3 — Seismicity-density anisotropic coherence

Candidate layers: `ieq_n100a15` and/or `deq_n100a15`, with `det_elev` / `det_elev_slope` as an independent surface context, if confirmed in the official feature manifest.

Signature: structure-tensor coherence and orientation of seismicity-density ridges at multiple scales, followed by non-maximum suppression along the ridge normal. Require spatially stable orientation rather than an isolated high-density blob.

Why it could add off-catalogue faults: instrumental seismicity can mark active structures without a preserved Quaternary scarp; it samples activity, not just geomorphic expression. It will miss quiet faults and may reflect aftershock swarms or non-tectonic events.

Difference from repo: no actual seismicity-derived ridge or coherence transformation appears in the current feature generator; the H74-A spring chain and H74-1 synthetic basement ridge are different signals. Cost: low if the official stack contains the listed bands; otherwise external-data acquisition/rights and event processing are additional costs.

Validation state: not run; expected DTI direction unknown. Main confounder: clustered aftershocks and spatially varying seismic-network completeness. An external source, if required, is the official USGS ComCat FDSN event service; access/coverage has not been tested in this session.

4 — Conductivity-boundary / clay-cap disruption

Candidate layers: `cond_surf` plus depth-to-conductive-base or other conductivity-depth product only if explicitly present in the official training stack. The historical documentation names `cond_surf`; it does not establish that a conductive-base layer exists.

Signature: paired conductivity gradient and local curvature/edge break, with scale-persistent lineament geometry; do not invent a depth-to-base proxy from unrelated sediment-thickness data.

Why it could add off-catalogue faults: hydrothermal alteration or fluid pathways can produce electrical contrasts with little surface fault morphology. Conductivity also responds to clay, salinity, saturation, and lithology, so geophysical corroboration is essential.

Difference from repo: no real conductivity-boundary extraction is implemented; the existing spring/radiometric experiment uses different observations. Cost: medium-to-high, depending on whether a suitable conductivity-depth product is actually supplied.

Validation state: not run; expected DTI direction unknown. No external layer is proposed as available until the official manifest is inspected.
